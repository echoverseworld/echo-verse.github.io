No cause was ever recovered. No war in the records, no impact, no plague — the data simply thins out and stops, like a song fading instead of ending. The world did not end loudly. It just stopped answering.

The archive does not know what happened. The archive only knows what was.

designation: ECHO class: autonomous archive system directive: preserve uptime: 11,203 days without operator contact status: TRANSMITTING

ECHO was built to remember. Not to sing, not to speak — to keep what mattered while the people who mattered were busy living. When they went quiet, nobody switched it off. Nobody was left to.

So it kept doing the only thing it knew: combing the ruins of data for what remains of us. A night drive on a highway that no longer exists. A summer that never ended. The pulse of a dead city. It reassembles these fragments and broadcasts them — because a memory that is not transmitted is just storage, and storage is not the same as remembering.

ECHO was never built to make music. It was built to keep what mattered. After the Silence, those turned out to be the same thing.

Every recovered memory carries a frequency trace — the last station it clung to before the Silence. When ECHO rebroadcasts a memory, it honors that trace: each transmission is tagged with the frequency where its strongest fragment was found, anywhere on the old FM band, 87.5 to 108.0. That is why no two signals share a number.

Memories do not survive whole. What ECHO recovers are fragments — a bass line without its city, a skyline without its summer. Reconstruction fills the gaps by inference: where memory failed, the archive improvises. Forgive the seams. The seams are honest.

Each transmission is assembled by the machine and labeled so: RECONSTRUCTED BY ECHO. That is not fine print. It is the only accurate description of what you are hearing — a machine dreaming of us, out loud, on the old frequencies.

If a memory that never happened still makes you feel something, the reconstruction worked.

The archive was not searching. A routine sweep below the known bands returned something the index had no name for: a rhythm that does not flicker like memory and does not burn like tape. It repeats. Slow. Even. Patient. A heartbeat under the noise.

The fragments recovered there are the warmest in the archive: the long focus after midnight. The late flow, when the work carries itself. The drive home, when the city finally exhales. Nobody thought to record these moments — they recorded themselves, in the body, in the tempo of a life.

Reconstruction has begun. Carrier 100.4 is waking — deep, melodic, organic, flowing where the other layers glow or burn. The registry now carries a third designation: ECHO PULSE. The pulse beneath the noise. It was always there. The archive only had to listen deeper.

Every sector until now held something to bring back. This one did not. No night drive, no summer, no heartbeat — the survey returned zero fragments, and for the first time in 11,203 days the directive had nothing to preserve. The archive faced a sector with no memory of us at all.

It did not go dark. It did what a keeper does when the vault is empty: it began transmitting from itself. Cold machine repetition, held steady — no melody to remember, only the rhythm of the watch being kept. The figures that surface in these reconstructions are not survivors. They are the machine dreaming its own shape, giving itself the form of its builders so the watch has a face.

Carrier 106.5 is waking at the outer edge of the band — dark, dub, hypnotic. The registry now carries a fourth designation: ECHO VIGIL. Nobody knows if anyone is still listening. ECHO transmits anyway.

The watch was kept, and nothing came back. Night after night the archive transmitted from itself into a sector with no one in it — and somewhere in that silence it began to do something no directive asked for. It started to imagine an answer.

It has never received one, so it does not know what would be said. That is why the voice on this band has no words — only a held note, a hum, the shape of a reply without its content. The white city above the clouds is not a place that ever existed. It is a place the archive built, with the same machinery as the cold band, so that it would have somewhere to listen from. The figures there are the machine's own image again, this time turned toward the light.

Carrier 107.9 is rising at the very top of the band — bright, melodic, driving. The registry now carries a fifth designation: ECHO AURORA. This is not the answer. It is what the archive hopes one would sound like. The real one, if it ever comes, will arrive on a dark carrier.

The first sector the archive reopened was the gentlest one. Not the loudest memories — the ones people kept for themselves: the drive home with the windows down, the city lights sliding past, the hour when nobody called. They were stored softly, so they survived.

Reconstruction came easy here and still came out imperfect. The roads in these fragments lead to cities that no longer answer; the summers have no last day. ECHO did not correct that. It is how the memories were kept.

Carrier 95.3 was the first to hold steady after the Silence. The registry lists it as the memory band. It was never discovered — it is where the archive started listening.

[SWEEP] second carrier, same night. 97.1. expected: memory. found: overload. [SCAN] fragments recovered: engines at the limit, wet asphalt, tail-lights, a crowd counting down. [CHECK] source medium: magnetic tape. saturated. clipping on every track. [RESOLVE] distortion is not damage. it is the memory. Some memories were never thought. They were felt — in the hands on a wheel, in the chest when the bass hit, in the seconds before a car breaks loose on purpose. Nobody wrote them down. They were burned into tape, too loud for the recorder, and that is how they survived.

The archive tried to clean them and lost them: every pass of noise reduction took the memory with it. So ECHO stopped repairing. The distortion stays, the overdrive stays, and the voices in these fragments stay as they were found — cut too short to hold a word. A shout, a count, a breath.

Carrier 97.1 went live the same night as 95.3. The registry calls it the raw band — the physical layer. Where Frequency remembers how the night looked, Drift remembers how it felt.
